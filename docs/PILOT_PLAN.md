# RowForge customer-onboarding pilot

Status: unvalidated business hypothesis. No customers, revenue, interviews, or commitments claimed.

## First buyer and offer

Target founder-led B2B scheduling software companies with recurring customer CSV migrations. The buyer is the founder or implementation lead. Test a fixed-price, $300 pilot for one customer file, one agreed output schema, and one revision. This is a proposed test price, not proven willingness to pay. Do not promise appointment migrations or cross-file relationship checks yet.

Deliver a reviewed output file, rejected-row report, and reconciliation of input/accepted/rejected/duplicate counts. Customer approves the output before importing into their system. The current product renames columns, trims whitespace, validates required/email/numeric fields, and removes exact valid duplicates. It does not write into a customer's application.

## Discovery before expanding

Find 20 relevant companies through public software directories and company websites. Record company, source URL, buyer role, evidence of CSV onboarding, contact channel, and interview status. Do not invent contacts or send unsolicited messages automatically.

Interview ten potential buyers. Ask about their most recent migration, actual files involved, time spent, who did the work, errors that delayed onboarding, current tools, and purchasing authority. Ask to see a redacted example and destination specification. Avoid asking whether they merely like the idea.

Proceed to paid pilots if at least three describe recurring manual work and two agree to a paid, clearly scoped trial. These are decision thresholds, not forecasts. If interest stays hypothetical, change the buyer or problem before adding features.

Draft outreach for the founder to review and send:

> I'm developing a tool for customer spreadsheet migrations into scheduling software. I'm researching how teams handle column mapping and validation today. Could I ask about your last migration in a 15-minute call? This is an early project; I'm looking for specific problems rather than pitching a finished service.

## Pilot scorecard

Track agreed scope, price, source row count, time spent by operator, customer time saved with an explicit baseline, corrections requested, processing costs, acceptance, and whether the buyer needs another migration. Collect customer permission before using any testimonial or results publicly.

Two paid pilots prove a small amount of willingness to pay; repeat purchases and lower operator time are the next tests. Avoid treating custom services revenue as proof of scalable software economics.

## Budget and operating constraints

Keep discovery and synthetic demos local initially: $0 additional hosting or AI API spend. Proposed monthly ceiling after pilot demand: $40 hosting/storage/backups, $20 optional inference, $10 domain/email reserve, $30 buffer. These are spending caps, not provider quotes. No purchases are authorized or made by this plan.

Before accepting real customer files, resolve hosting security, backups, deletion/retention, account recovery, access restrictions, and a written scope/data handling agreement. Start with redacted, non-sensitive samples. Current local prototype is not a production customer-data service.

## Next product work, gated by evidence

1. Confirm actual source files and destination schema with pilot buyers.
2. Add a guided mapping review and reusable destination templates.
3. Add multi-file references only if actual migrations need them.
4. Add delivery/reconciliation exports and measure review time.
5. Deploy a secured pilot workspace after reviewing data requirements.

Funding is a later option, not a launch dependency. Evidence worth collecting includes repeat usage, measurable onboarding improvements, customer acquisition cost, gross margin including founder labor, and a path to a larger repeatable market. No funding outcome is guaranteed.
